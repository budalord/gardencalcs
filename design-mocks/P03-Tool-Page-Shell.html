How it works

From soil sample to amendment amount in four steps

The same rhythm every calculator on this site follows — inputs, formula, citation, answer.

1

Measure your current pH

Use a soil test kit or send a sample to your state Extension office. Sample in 3–5 spots, mix, and read.

2

Enter the numbers

Current pH, target pH, soil type, and the area you plan to amend. All four are required for an accurate rate.

3

Read the amendment rate

The calculator picks lime or sulfur automatically and shows lbs and kg. Safe-range warnings surface when needed.

4

Apply and retest

Incorporate to 6-inch depth, water in, and retest in 6–8 weeks. Lime is slow — don't re-dose before then.

Example 1 · Vegetable garden

Amending a 100 sqft clay bed from pH 5.5 to 6.5

+

Example 2 · Blueberry bed

Lowering a 32 sqft raised bed from pH 7.5 to 6.5

+
  • Current pH7.5
  • Target pH (acidic crops)6.5
  • Soil type → factorloamy = 0.05
  • Area32 sqft
  • Amendment (target < current → sulfur)(7.5 − 6.5) × 32 × 0.05 × 0.56
Apply 0.9 lbs of elemental sulfur, incorporated. Retest in 2–3 months (sulfur is slower than lime).

Frequently asked

Questions we get after the calculation

How often should I test my soil pH? +
Test in-ground beds once per year, ideally in the fall after harvest so amendments have the winter to react. Raised beds with fresh compost inputs benefit from a re-test every six months — they drift faster. Cornell Cooperative Extension · Soil pH Testing · 2022
Can I use wood ash to raise pH instead of lime? +
Yes, cautiously. Wood ash is roughly half as effective as agricultural lime and adds significant potassium — which can be undesirable at high rates. Limit applications to 20 lbs per 1,000 sqft per year. Oregon State Extension · EC 1503 · 2018
Does tap water drift my soil pH over time? +
Only if your municipal water is strongly alkaline (above pH 8.0). For most U.S. tap water, the effect is negligible over a single season. Container plantings are more sensitive than in-ground beds. UGA Extension · Bulletin 1579 · 2020
What's the difference between pelletized and powdered lime? +
Both are calcium carbonate. Pelletized is dust-free and spreader-friendly; powdered reacts faster. For equivalent amounts, powdered shifts pH 1–2 months sooner. The calculator uses a neutral factor that's accurate for either. Penn State Extension · Soil Management · 2021
Why does my container soil read so different from my in-ground soil? +
Container mixes are typically peat- or coir-based and often start at pH 5.5–6.0 from the bag — well below most in-ground soils. Re-amend containers at repotting rather than every season. UMass Extension · Container & Raised-Bed Soil Management · 2019

Methodology & sources

The rate factor by soil type (sandy 0.03, loamy 0.05, clay 0.08) comes from Cornell Cooperative Extension. The base conversion is calibrated against University of Massachusetts soil test reports. All formulas are visible below; the calculator is client-side and stores no input.

amount = |target_pH − current_pH| × area_sqft × factor[soil] × k[amendment]
