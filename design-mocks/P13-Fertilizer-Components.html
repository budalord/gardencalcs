Annotated fertilizer bag · what the label actually says

Anatomy of a fertilizer label

Five things on the bag that matter. Everything else is marketing.

1Brand name
Marketing. Ignore. The brand doesn't change the chemistry.
2Grade — N-P-K by weight percent
10-10-10 means 10 % nitrogen, 10 % available phosphate (P₂O₅), 10 % soluble potash (K₂O). The third number isn't pure potassium, and the three don't add to 100 — the rest is filler and carrier.
3Guaranteed analysis
Legally required. This is the only claim you can hold the manufacturer to.
4Derived from
The actual ingredients. "Urea" vs "blood meal" tells you organic vs synthetic, release speed, and whether it burns plants on contact.
5Net weight
Use this, not "coverage," to compute dose. 40 lb of 10-10-10 contains 4 lb N total.

Deficiency symptom cards · what yellow leaves are telling you

Reading the leaf

Three common macronutrient deficiencies — where they show first and what they look like.

N
Nitrogen

Shows on older, lower leaves first

  • Uniform pale green → yellow
  • Stunted overall growth
  • Purple-tinged stems in brassicas
P
Phosphorus

Shows on older leaves & stems

  • Dull, dark purple-green
  • Reddish undersides on tomato
  • Poor root & flower development
K
Potassium

Shows on older leaf edges first

  • Browned, scorched leaf margins
  • Yellowing between veins
  • Weak stems, blotchy fruit

Confirm with a soil test before fertilizing — visual symptoms can overlap with pH lockout, root damage, or disease. Source: Clemson HGIC 1254, UMass Ext.

Organic vs synthetic — compared on one card

Which kind to buy

Same two pounds of nitrogen, different delivery. Trade-offs laid out side by side.

Organic · blood meal, compost, fish

Release speed
Weeks to months — microbes must break it down
N per dollar
$3–6 per lb N (higher)
Soil health
Feeds microbes & OM; builds long-term structure
Burn risk
Low · very hard to over-apply
Cold-weather use
Poor — microbes dormant under 50 °F
Runoff risk
Lower (bound to OM)

Synthetic · urea, ammonium, NPK blends

Release speed
Days — immediately plant-available
N per dollar
$1–2 per lb N (lower)
Soil health
Neutral to slightly negative over decades
Burn risk
High · over-application damages roots
Cold-weather use
Good — doesn't need biological activity
Runoff risk
Higher (soluble, mobile)

Most Extension services recommend a hybrid: organic for soil building, synthetic spot-dressing during peak fruiting. Source: Penn State Ext. Agronomy Guide; Rodale Institute trials.
